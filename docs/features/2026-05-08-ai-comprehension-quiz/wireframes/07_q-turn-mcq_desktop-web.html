Which of the following best describes loss aversion as Kahneman frames it?

Your answer (revealed)
A. People weigh losses about half as heavily as equivalent gains.
B. People weigh losses roughly twice as heavily as equivalent gains. ✓
C. People weigh gains and losses roughly equally.
D. People are indifferent to losses below a personal threshold.
Agent feedback
  • What was right: you picked B — the loss-multiplier framing.
  • What was missing: Kahneman pins the multiplier at roughly 2× (sometimes 1.5–2.5×) — worth holding the rough magnitude, not just the direction.
  • From the source: Chapter 26 frames loss aversion as an asymmetry built into prospect theory's value function — losses bend the curve more steeply than gains.
