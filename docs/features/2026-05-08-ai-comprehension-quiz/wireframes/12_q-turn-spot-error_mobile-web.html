…loss aversion …weigh losses about half as heavily as equivalent gains. What's wrong with this statement?

Your answer

Losses are weighed roughly twice as heavily as gains, not half. The statement inverts the ratio.

Agent: correct
The flaw

The ratio is inverted (loss-aversion coefficient ≈ 2). Losses are weighed about twice as heavily as equivalent gains, not half.

Why this matters

The asymmetry is what makes prospect theory predict status-quo bias and the endowment effect. Halve the coefficient and the theory collapses into expected-utility.
