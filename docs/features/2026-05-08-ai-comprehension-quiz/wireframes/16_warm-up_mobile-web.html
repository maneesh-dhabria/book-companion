Open-ended Source: Chapter 12

Imagine someone overestimates the risk of shark attacks after a news cycle full of beach incidents. Explain in your own words what cognitive shortcut is at play and why it leads people astray.

Your answer

When something is easy to recall — like recent shark attack headlines — people treat it as more frequent than it really is. They mistake what comes to mind quickly for what is statistically common.

Agent verdict: Got it Chapter 12 · pp. 129–131

Yes — that's the availability heuristic: judging frequency or probability by the ease with which examples come to mind. Recent, vivid, emotionally charged events are over-weighted. Your shark-attack framing nails the mechanism. To strengthen it: notice that the same shortcut runs in the opposite direction — events that are hard to recall feel rarer than they are.
